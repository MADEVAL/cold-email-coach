# The 11-point checklist

Use this for REVIEW and to self-check WRITE and REWRITE. Every item is binary: PASS or FAIL. A PASS needs no explanation unless the reason is non-obvious. A FAIL always gets a one-line reason.

## 1. Length

**PASS:** body is 50–125 words.
**FAIL:** under 50 (feels abrupt) or over 125 (loses phone readers).

Count the body, not the subject or signature. If the email is 126 words, cut. Do not round down.

## 2. First line

**PASS:** the first line is about the recipient, their work, or their problem.
**FAIL:** the first line is about the sender, the company, or the sender's manners.

Examples:

- PASS: "Your post about week-one drop-off matched what we see in most onboarding data."
- FAIL: "I hope this email finds you well."
- FAIL: "My name is Alex and I'm the founder of..."
- FAIL: "I wanted to reach out because we help companies like yours."

A placeholder such as `[PERSONALIZE: ...]` is a PASS for honesty and a TODO for sending. Say so.

## 3. No pitch

**PASS:** at most one sentence of context about what you do, with no feature list.
**FAIL:** a list of capabilities, a company biography, or self-praise.

- PASS: "We make a checklist tool for CS teams."
- FAIL: "Our AI-powered, end-to-end platform integrates with your stack, automates follow-ups, and provides real-time analytics."

## 4. Problem first

**PASS:** the email names a concrete problem or observation the recipient recognizes.
**FAIL:** the email asserts quality, leadership, or transformation without a problem.

- PASS: "When a new account goes quiet in week one, nobody notices until the renewal call."
- FAIL: "We're the leading onboarding platform for modern SaaS teams."

## 5. One CTA

**PASS:** exactly one ask, phrased as a question with a low bar for answering.
**FAIL:** zero asks, two or more asks, or a high-bar ask such as a 30-minute call as the first step.

- PASS: "Is this something you're working on?"
- PASS: "Worth a look, or not a priority?"
- FAIL: "Would you be open to a 30-minute demo or, if not, can you reply with your thoughts?"
- FAIL: "Let me know if you'd like to chat. Also, here's my calendar link."

## 6. You over me

**PASS:** "you/your" appears more often than "I/me/my/we/our".
**FAIL:** the sender is the grammatical center of the email.

Count pronouns. The company name also counts as sender language.

- PASS: "Your team ... you ... your accounts ... we built ... you"
- FAIL: "We ... our platform ... we ... our clients ... we"

## 7. Plain language

**PASS:** simple words, short sentences, average sentence length under 15 words, no jargon.
**FAIL:** corporate filler, abstractions, acronyms the recipient did not introduce.

Watch for: leverage, utilize, facilitate, optimize, streamline, robust, seamless, end-to-end, synergy, solution, ecosystem, paradigm.

## 8. Subject line

**PASS:** 1–6 words, neutral, sentence case or lowercase, no hype, no caps, no exclamation marks.
**FAIL:** long, salesy, clickbait, all caps, emoji, fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".

- PASS: `week one`
- PASS: `a question about onboarding`
- FAIL: `🔥 TRANSFORM YOUR ONBOARDING TODAY!!!`
- FAIL: `Quick question about your 2026 customer success strategy`

## 9. No clichés

**PASS:** no template openings or filler phrases.
**FAIL:** any phrase from `references/anti-patterns.md`, including:

- "Hope this email finds you well"
- "I wanted to reach out"
- "I know you're busy"
- "Just following up"
- "Circling back"
- "Quick call"
- "Pick your brain"
- "At your earliest convenience"

## 10. No pressure

**PASS:** no deadlines, scarcity, exaggeration, or promised results.
**FAIL:** "last chance", "act now", "limited spots", "guaranteed 3x pipeline", "this will change everything".

Real constraints are allowed if true: "the pilot closes on Friday" is fine when it is a fact.

## 11. Human voice

**PASS:** it can be read aloud without cringing. It sounds like one person wrote it to another.
**FAIL:** it sounds assembled, over-polished, or like it was written to a segment rather than a person.

Test: read it aloud. If you would not say a sentence to a colleague, cut it.

## Scoring

- **10–11:** send it. Check facts and the personalization one last time.
- **8–9:** small fixes. Usually length, a second ask, or one cliché.
- **6–7:** rewrite the opening and the ask. The middle will follow.
- **5 or below:** start over. The email is about the sender, not the recipient.

The score is a diagnostic, not a guarantee. A technically clean email can still be irrelevant. Relevance is judged against the recipient, not the checklist.

## Review etiquette

- Be specific. "Too long" is not a fix. "Cut the second paragraph and the calendar link; the email drops from 190 to 96 words" is.
- Limit fixes to three. More than three means rewrite, not patch.
- Do not rewrite in REVIEW mode unless asked.
- Do not praise the email to soften the critique. The user wants the gap, not reassurance.
