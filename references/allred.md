# Will Allred and Lavender: the mechanics of a reply

Will Allred's work at Lavender focuses on the details that decide whether an email gets read: length, readability, the first line, the number of asks, and the ratio of "you" to "I".

The notes below are a practical summary for this skill. They are not quotes, and this project is not affiliated with Will Allred or Lavender.

## Length

- Target **50-125 words** for the body.
- Under 50 words can feel abrupt unless the context is already warm.
- Over 125 words loses phone readers. If the email needs scrolling, it needs editing.
- One paragraph can be one or two sentences. Three or four short paragraphs are enough.

Why it works: the recipient decides in a few seconds whether the email is worth their attention. Length is the first signal.

## Readability

- Aim for a grade **3-5** reading level. Short words, short sentences.
- Average sentence length: under 15 words.
- Replace multi-syllable abstractions with concrete nouns.
- One idea per sentence.
- Read it aloud. If you run out of breath, cut it.

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "leverage" | "use" |
| "utilize" | "use" |
| "facilitate" | "help" |
| "optimize" | "improve" |
| "streamline" | "simplify" |
| "robust solution" | "a tool that works" |
| "end-to-end platform" | "one place for X" |
| "drive business outcomes" | "get more accounts live" |

## The first line

The first line earns the second line. It should be about the recipient, not the sender.

Good openings:

- A specific observation about their work.
- A question about their process.
- A relevant event or change at their company.
- A shared context that is real.

Weak openings:

- "Hope this email finds you well."
- "My name is X and I work at Y."
- "I wanted to reach out because..."
- "I know you're busy, so I'll keep this short."
- "As a [role], you know how important [topic] is."

If you do not have a real personalization fact, use a placeholder. Do not fake it.

## One idea, one ask

- Every additional idea reduces the chance of a reply.
- Every additional CTA splits attention.
- The ask should be a question with a low cost of answering.
- Do not stack "book a call" and "reply with your thoughts". Pick one.

## You versus I

- "You/your" should outnumber "I/me/my/we/our".
- The company name should appear zero or one time.
- Count the words. If "we" is winning, rewrite from the recipient's side of the table.

Weak:

> We are excited to share that our platform now includes a new dashboard. We believe it will help teams like yours.

Better:

> Your team probably checks three places before a new account goes live. The dashboard puts that in one.

## Subject lines

- Short: 1-5 words is ideal, 6 is the practical ceiling.
- Neutral: sentence case or lowercase.
- No hype, no all caps, no exclamation marks, no emoji.
- No fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
- Curiosity is fine. Clickbait is not.
- Avoid spam-trigger words: free, guarantee, urgent, act now, limited time, risk-free, 100%.

Examples:

- `week one`
- `setup drop-off`
- `a question about onboarding`
- `stuck accounts`
- `before the renewal call`
- `handoff notes`

## Formatting

- Plain text.
- No images, banners, or heavy signatures.
- No bullet lists in the first email. They read like a pitch deck.
- One link at most, and only if it is genuinely useful.
- Signature: name and role, one line. No address, no logo, no social icons.

## Follow-ups

- A follow-up is a new attempt, not a reminder.
- Add one new thing: an angle, an example, a question, a useful link.
- Keep it shorter than the first email.
- Two or three follow-ups are enough. Then stop.
- Never guilt the recipient. "Just bumping this" is a waste of a send.

## Measurement

- Track replies, not opens. Open tracking is unreliable because of privacy features and can hurt deliverability.
- A reply that says "not now" is still a reply. It is information.
- Do not optimize for meetings booked from a single email. Optimize for a conversation started.
- Test one variable at a time: opener, ask, or length. Not all three.
