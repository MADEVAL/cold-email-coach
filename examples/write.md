# Example: WRITE

> All names, companies, products, and numbers in this example are fictional. They exist to show the format. Never copy them into a real email.

## The brief

- **Recipient:** Dana Ruiz, Head of Customer Success at Northwind, a 120-person B2B SaaS company.
- **Offer:** Relay, a small checklist tool for onboarding.
- **Problem:** new accounts stall in week one and the CS team chases them by hand.
- **Personalization:** Dana published a post saying 40% of new accounts never finish setup.
- **Goal:** a reply, not a meeting.
- **Language:** English.

Good brief: recipient, offer, problem, and a real personalization fact. No questions needed.

## Variant 1 — observation

**Subject:** week one

Dana — your post about 40% of new accounts never finishing setup stayed with me. That number usually lives in a spreadsheet, and someone ends up chasing each account by hand.

We make a small checklist tool for CS teams. It shows where each account gets stuck, so nobody has to guess.

Is that still a problem for you, or is setup under control?

Yevhen, Founder at Relay

**Words:** 67

## Variant 2 — problem question

**Subject:** setup drop-off

Dana — when a new account goes quiet in week one, who notices first: your team, or nobody?

Most CS teams find out at the renewal call. We built a checklist tool that flags stuck accounts on day three instead of day thirty.

Is that relevant, or already solved on your side?

Yevhen, Founder at Relay

**Words:** 55

## Variant 3 — short case

**Subject:** day three, not day fourteen

Dana — if your team finds stuck accounts at the two-week review, that is usually a week too late. One CS lead we work with now sees them on day three, with the same accounts and an earlier conversation.

We make the checklist tool behind that. No dashboard overhaul, just a short daily list.

Does that match how your team works, or is this already handled?

Yevhen, Founder at Relay

**Words:** 69

Variant 3 uses a case. It is acceptable only because a real one exists in this fictional brief. If there is no real case, skip this variant. Do not invent a customer.

## Why these work

- The first line is a fact about Dana's world, not a compliment.
- The product appears in one sentence, after the problem.
- Each email ends with a question that costs one word to answer.
- The signature is one line. No banner, no calendar link.
- The three variants use different entries so the user can choose a tone, not just wording.
